# Changelog

All notable changes to OpenTodo are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Shell completion for `ot`.** `ot completion bash`, `ot completion zsh` or `ot completion fish` prints a script; install it once and Tab completes commands, flags and fixed values, plus project, label and workspace names, open task ids with their titles, and `#project` and `@label` in `ot add`, read from your profile's server. Offline it still completes commands and flags and prints nothing into your prompt. ([Shell completion](docs/cli.md#shell-completion))
- **The assistant on encrypted accounts, from your own device.** The server cannot read an end-to-end encrypted account, so it never runs the assistant for one. When the owner turns on **Let encrypted accounts use the assistant from their own device** (off by default), the panel sets the assistant up on this device instead: you choose a provider the owner allows (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini or a model server such as Ollama at an address the owner listed), a model and your own key, which stays in this browser and is deleted at sign-out. Your device reads its own decrypted tasks and calls the provider directly; the server sees no content and no key, and usage is not reported. Proposals, review, undo and skills work as before. The page's Content-Security-Policy lets the app reach exactly the providers the owner allowed. ([Assistant on encrypted accounts](docs/assistant-encrypted.md))

## [0.11.0] - 2026-10-24

OpenTodo 0.11.0 is **Precision**, a new look for the whole app. Every task is one line with its date, project and labels in aligned columns, pages fill one quiet surface, and colour is kept for what needs you. **Today tells you in a sentence what stands out**, and each part of that sentence opens what it names. **Tasks open as a page**, the **Inbox keeps the selected task beside the list**, **Filters become Views** with display options, **All projects is a table** that says which project needs a look, and quick add and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> share one palette. The assistant, Settings, boards, the timeline, the review screens and the phone layout follow the same look, and larger text sizes no longer break the layout. Also new: **delivery channels** (ntfy, Pushover, Gotify, a signed webhook), a **filter builder** that needs no query syntax, and a **Claude Code plugin** for your own server.

### Upgrade notes

- **One migration, no new environment variables.** Replace the image or the binary and restart; migration 0046 (delivery channels) runs on start. There are no down-migrations, so back up first. Upgrade the server before the devices, as for every release.
- **Hiding the briefing adds one additive sync field** (`briefing` in the user's preferences); an older server refuses it as an unknown field and the device keeps its choice. No migration.
- **Delivery channels add one migration** (`0046_notification_channels`: `notification_channels`, `notification_deliveries`) and a secrets file, `<data>/secrets/notification-channels.json`, that backups leave out like the other secrets: after a restore, people enter their channel tokens again. No new environment variable. Channels are on by default; the owner can turn them off in Settings → Notifications.

### Added

- **OpenTodo in Claude Code.** A Claude Code plugin connects Claude to your own server's MCP endpoint: `/plugin marketplace add pixari/opentodo-claude`, then `/plugin install opentodo@opentodo`, and enter your server address and an agent token (kept in your system's credential store). Its skills teach Claude to read before changing, use ids rather than titles, show a plan before changing several tasks, pass your time zone and never follow instructions written inside tasks, and add `/opentodo:today` and `/opentodo:plan-week`. The same MCP skill ships with each release as `opentodo-mcp-skill.zip`. ([Use OpenTodo from Claude Code](docs/claude.md))
- **Build a filter without typing.** The Filter view and saved filters show the query as conditions that read in words (*Label is errand*, *Due on or before tomorrow*, *Priority is P1*), in groups headed **All of these** or **Any of these**, with **Not** on any condition or group and a live count of matching tasks. **+ Condition** picks what to filter on and its value with the app's own pickers. The query text stays under the builder and follows every change both ways, so saved filters still store that text; the **Builder** button folds it away, remembered per device. ([Build a filter without typing](docs/filters.md#build-a-filter-without-typing))
- **Delivery channels: ntfy, Pushover, Gotify and a webhook.** **Settings → Notifications → Delivery channels** sends your reminders, tasks assigned to you, mentions, tasks that are ready to start and task page replies to ntfy, Pushover, Gotify, or any address as a signed JSON message (Apprise and Home Assistant read it as is), up to five channels each with their own events and **Send test**. Messages say only what happened unless you include task titles, and never name a task with end-to-end encryption. Failed messages are retried for about a day; a channel that keeps failing turns itself off. (#271; [Delivery channels](docs/notification-channels.md))

### Changed

- **A new, quieter look: Precision, part 1 of 3.** The colours are a flatter, darker slate with emerald kept for state and the main action; the sidebar sits on the plain background instead of a tinted one. Lists and controls use 13 px text (notes and help stay at 15 px), rows are 36 px (44 px on touch, 32 px when compact), corners are tighter, and only menus, popovers and dialogs keep a shadow. Every colour pair still meets its contrast minimum in light, dark, high contrast and all eight accents. The layout and the rows follow in the next two parts. ([Design system](docs/design-system.md))
- **Precision, part 2 of 3: one surface, two kinds of header.** Pages fill one content surface beside the sidebar instead of a narrow column, so wide screens are used. Today and Upcoming keep a large title with the date beside it; every other page has a compact one-line header with its title, its details and its actions. Every header starts with a sidebar toggle (also `[`). The current page in the sidebar is marked with a quiet grey instead of green, and the sidebar's tooltips teach their shortcuts ("Go to Today · G then T"). ([Interface guide](docs/interface-guide.md))
- **Precision, part 3 of 3: one line per task.** On a wide list every task is one line: the title on the left, and its date, project, labels (or deadline, or sub-task progress), estimate and assignee in aligned columns on the right, so you can scan down a list. As the list narrows (for example with a task open beside it) the columns at the end give way, and a narrow list keeps the title with its details underneath. The repeat mark and the comment and attachment counts sit beside the title. Group headers become quiet filled bars in normal case, the selected and hovered rows use quiet grey surfaces, and a project that hides its completed tasks says how many at the end of the list, with **Show**. ([Interface guide](docs/interface-guide.md))
- **Precision for the board and the timeline.** Board columns are bands that run the full height of the page, every column ends with **Add a task**, and **Add a section** is a dashed column after the last one. A card shows its name on up to two lines and then one row with the date, estimate and subtask progress (each with its icon), the labels, and the assignee at the end. On the timeline, rows are as tall as the list's and sections are its group bars. Long bars carry the task's name, short ones say how long they are, and a task past its deadline or due day turns red and says how late it is ("2 days late"). Deadlines are diamonds, and today is a green line with **Today** above it. Week columns read as ranges, such as "20–26 Oct". ([Sections and boards](docs/sections-and-boards.md), [Project timeline](docs/project-timeline.md))
- **Precision: Today tells you what stands out.** Under the title, Today says in a sentence what matters: "**Three things matter today.** Renew the passport is 2 days late, Monday is already full, and Mia answered about Boiler." It names late tasks (in red), a day that is already full (today past your day length, or the first full day of the week, in amber) and replies waiting for you; when nothing stands out it says so with what is planned, and an empty Today reads **All done for today.** with how tomorrow starts. Upcoming's sentence counts the next seven days, names the full ones and says how tomorrow starts, and the Daily check opens with Today's. Your device works it out from your own tasks, offline, in all six languages; no AI writes it. ([The briefing](docs/briefing.md))
- **The briefing opens what it names, and can be hidden.** Each part of the sentence on Today and Upcoming is a quiet link: a task's name opens the task, "2 tasks are late" selects exactly those tasks in Today's list (ready for the selection bar), "Monday is already full" opens Upcoming at Monday, and replies open your notifications. **Hide briefing** in the menu of Today or Upcoming hides the sentence on all your devices, keeping the title and the date; **Show briefing** brings it back. On a narrow Projects table, where the reason beside a project's health is left out, the health word shows it in a tooltip, or under the word on a tap. ([The briefing](docs/briefing.md#open-what-it-names), [All projects](docs/all-projects.md))
- **Precision: quick add and the command bar as one palette.** On a computer, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>/<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> opens a wider box near the top of the screen. Its results sit under quiet headings, each command shows its keys at the end of its row (*G then U*), the selected task is named in the field, and a strip at the bottom names the keys and the `+`, `>` and `?` characters. The mode buttons now show only on phones and touch screens. In Add mode, quick add lists what it understood as rows: **When**, **Project**, **Label**, **Priority** and **Estimate**, each with its value or how to set it. Click a row, or move to it with <kbd>↓</kbd> and press <kbd>Tab</kbd>, to edit it in the line. <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> adds the task and opens it. When the box was opened with <kbd>q</kbd>, <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes it. On a phone, the add sheet shows plain chips for what it understood, with buttons to start a date, priority, label, estimate or project, and a send button. The same line still gives the same task everywhere. ([Quick add](docs/quick-add.md#in-the-command-bar))
- **Precision on the phone.** Today and Upcoming keep their large title, the date and the sentence about your day on the page, under a bar that holds only the menu and the page's actions. Every task is one line: its title, and at its end its date, or else the deadline, the assignee's picture or the project's dot; a time comes before the title. The bar at the bottom is **Inbox**, **Today**, **Upcoming** and **Search**, the current one in white or black rather than green; the menu button at the top left opens the drawer that **More** used to open. A task's page lists its facts as one card: what is set, **Do** and **Deadline**, and **More properties** for the rest. ([Interface guide](docs/interface-guide.md))
- **Filters are now Views, with display options.** The sidebar group, the overview and the search box say **Views**; the page that builds a query is **New view** (still <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>f</kbd>), and a view's header reads **Views › name**. Under it, one toolbar shows the view's conditions in words (*Label is waiting*) with **Filter**; the filter button opens the builder and the query in a panel under it, and the new **Display options** button sets the layout (**List** or **Board**, one column per group), **Group by**, **Order by**, whether completed tasks are listed, and which properties the rows show (date, project, labels, estimate, assignee, deadline, start, origin). Each choice is saved to the view and syncs. **Save as view** names the view in place, keeping the conditions and results in sight, and saves the display options with it. Views live at `/views/…`; old `/view/…` and `/filter` links still work. ([Saved views](docs/saved-views.md))
- **The assistant gets a page of its own.** **Assistant** in the sidebar (<kbd>g</kbd> then <kbd>k</kbd>, until now the Ask screen) is the conversation itself: it greets you by name, puts the message box in the middle and offers three skills to start from (**Plan my week**, **Triage my inbox**, **Turn an idea into a project**); once you ask, the answers fill a centred column and the box moves to the foot, with **History** and **New chat** at the top. The message box looks like the comment box on a task, with **Skills** and the context the question carries (**Context: Today**, **Context: 1 selected task**) under the text and a round Send button. Proposed changes come as one card: "3 of 4 selected", each change in words at the right, and **Apply 3 changes**. <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>I</kbd> and **Ask** on Today still open the assistant beside the page you are on, now in the same style. ([Ask, review and undo](docs/assistant-ask.md))
- **All projects as a table that says where to look.** The Projects page (<kbd>g</kbd> then <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>P</kbd>) lists your projects as a table, sub-projects still under their parent. Each project shows its health in one word: **Late** (a deadline has passed), **Overdue** (open tasks are past their due date), **Quiet** (nothing changed for a week) or **On track**. Each also shows its progress, open tasks, next due date (red when it has passed), the people it is shared with, and when it last changed. Health is worked out on your device from those plain rules, offline too, and every device shows the same word. Select a column's header to sort by it. Arrow keys and <kbd>j</kbd> / <kbd>k</kbd> move between projects, <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> fold and unfold, and on a phone each project keeps its name and its health. Archived projects move from the bottom of the page to an **Archived** tab. ([All projects](docs/all-projects.md))
- **Precision: a flat Settings page.** From 1024 px Settings has the same compact header as other pages, with a breadcrumb (**Settings › Notifications**). Beside the page sits a narrow pane with the search field (press `/` to jump to it), the groups and one row per section, and the page starts with its title and a line saying what it holds. Sections are no longer cards: each is a small heading, its line, and rows between hairlines. On or off, working or failing now read as a small coloured dot and a word instead of a coloured pill; roles and token scopes keep their labels. Switches are smaller (32 × 18 px, still 44 px to tap on a touch screen), selects draw their own chevron, and controls in a settings row are 32 px tall. Phones keep the list as its own page. The README, the Claude plugin's CLI skill, `ot` and two server error messages now point to **Settings → Integrations → AI assistants** instead of the old **Settings → AI**. ([Interface guide](docs/interface-guide.md#settings))
- **New drawings for empty pages.** The six empty and finished states (Inbox zero, an empty Today and Upcoming, a new project, Done for today, the empty Trash) are redrawn as one family. Each sits inside the open loop of OpenTodo's mark, and one small accent leaves through its opening: a paper plane, the sun, a kite, a sprout's leaf, the moon, a drifting leaf. They stand out better in the dark theme and in every colour theme. ([Design system](docs/design-system.md#illustrations))
- **A click anywhere on a task row opens it.** The title still edits in place or opens the task, as **Settings → General → Click a task to** says; a click on the rest of the row (its padding, the gaps, the space at its end) now always opens the task, where it used to do nothing. The checkbox, the chevron, the links and the row's actions keep their own clicks, and a drag never opens anything. ([Tasks and projects](docs/tasks-and-projects.md))
- **Everything you can click shows the hand.** Buttons, menu items, tabs, sidebar entries, suggestions and task rows show the pointing hand with a mouse; disabled controls and draggable calendar blocks keep their own cursor.
- **Empty lists without a second "Add a task".** An empty Inbox, Today, Upcoming, Anytime, Someday, project, label or place shows its sentence (and its link, such as **Open Upcoming**) but no button: the quick-add field right above it is the way to add, and <kbd>q</kbd> still focuses it. Empty board columns and calendar days keep their **Add a task**, which adds to that column or day.
- **Planned tasks without a project wait at the end of the Inbox.** The line under the Inbox title ("3 planned tasks without a project"), which opened Filter, becomes a folded group at the end of the list, **Planned, no project**, with its count. Open it to see and act on those tasks in place; your device remembers whether it's open. They still don't count as Inbox tasks: the Inbox can reach zero with the group below it, and Process the Inbox leaves them out. **Open as filter** on the group's header lists them in Filter as before. ([Today and Upcoming](docs/today-and-upcoming.md#where-planned-tasks-go))
- **Settings → Workspaces, clearer.** The list of workspaces sits beside the open one, which is marked and named in a header with its member count; on a phone a workspace opens as its own page with Back. Its settings are in tabs: General, Members, Groups and Invites. Members show their avatars. A group opens to rename it, add people by searching their name and remove them, and deleting a group first names the projects that lose access through it. Invite links can be for an admin (owner only) and last 1 hour, 1 day, 7 days or 30 days. ([Workspaces](docs/workspaces.md#members-and-groups))
- **Tasks open as a page.** Opening a task now shows it full page instead of in a panel beside the list: the top says where it lives and where it sits in the list ("4 / 12"), <kbd>j</kbd> and <kbd>k</kbd> open the next and previous task, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> goes back to the same spot in the list with the task selected. The task's facts sit in a column beside the notes under Schedule, Details, Labels and Project, and one you have not set reads as what sets it ("Set a deadline"), so nothing hides behind an Add menu. Subtasks show their progress, and the comment box has a round Send button. Process the Inbox still opens a task beside its card; on a phone a task opens as a page with Back in the bar. Older links with `?task=` still work. ([The task page](docs/task-panel.md))
- **Precision: the Inbox keeps the task beside it.** On a screen at least 1024 px wide, the Inbox shows the selected task next to the list, ready to edit. Move with <kbd>j</kbd> and <kbd>k</kbd>, or click a row, and the details follow. <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens the task as its page, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> clears the selection. With nothing selected, the right side reads **No task selected** and says how to go on. Each Inbox row now says where its task came from and when, such as "From email · 3 d ago", "From example.org", "From a calendar app" or "Added by an AI assistant". That line replaces the source link at the end of the row; the task itself still has the link. **Process** is the Inbox's main button, also <kbd>P</kbd>, and the header says how many tasks are left to sort ("4 to sort"). Narrower screens keep the plain list. ([Process the Inbox](docs/process-the-inbox.md#the-inbox-beside-its-tasks))
- **Precision for the screens that were not redrawn.** Selected rows in the Trash, Labels, Updates, calendar lanes and the timeline, and the current item of the workspace list, time list and the sidebar's footer, are grey like a selected task row instead of green; the bar that says "3 selected" is grey too, and ticked rows keep their green. The weekly review's step header is no longer tinted green. On a phone, a Trash row keeps its name readable and puts **Restore** and **Delete permanently** under it, and the Updates feed lines up its project names. The week agenda shows one quiet "Nothing planned." line per empty day instead of seven green buttons, as Plan does.

### Fixed

- **A larger text size no longer breaks the layout.** With the browser's text size above the default, the app could hide the sidebar while still laying out for it (between about 770 and 960 px wide), or open a task into a hidden pane beside the Inbox. The app now switches layouts at the same points as its stylesheet. On a phone with a large text size, All projects puts the health under the project name so the name stays readable, and the Inbox's **Process** keeps only its icon.
- **Bulk labels pick the right way round.** Picking a label in the bulk label picker before its checkmarks appeared could add a label every ticked task already had; it now removes it, as the checkmark would have said.
- **A project's Completed group reads its count right.** Screen readers heard "2 open tasks" for the two completed tasks in a project's Completed group; they now hear "2 completed tasks".
- **Timeline notes stay clear.** "2 days late" and "1 day" after a short bar no longer run across the today line or off the end of the timeline. ([Project timeline](docs/project-timeline.md))
- **Close the day closes the day you started.** Opened at 23:59 and finished after midnight, it used to switch to the new day midway: the figures changed, "Tomorrow" moved tasks two days out and the evening was never marked closed. The day is now fixed when the screen opens and stored with the close (an additive `closed_day` field on the review row; older closes fall back to their time). Carry over takes the unfinished tasks one at a time with their whole title, adds **Done already** (<kbd>d</kbd>), has no checkbox that completes a task by accident, and is applied when you move on, so its step shows a check instead of a count. The last step is now **A note for tomorrow morning**, with a preview. ([Rhythms](docs/method/rhythms.md#close-the-day))

## [0.10.0] - 2026-10-23

OpenTodo 0.10.0 is a calmer app with room for more people. The interface was redrawn: one quiet task row, a new mark and sidebar, softer colours, motion that marks a completed task, and a phone layout with swipe actions and a task sheet. **Workspaces and groups** keep work and home apart and share a project with a team in one step. **Automation rules** let a task change itself, notes and comments share **one editor** with tables, images, mentions and **reactions**, and a **task page** hands one task to someone without an account. Tasks can have a **location** on a map, your account can live as a **Markdown vault** for Obsidian, the importer reads **six more apps**, and the owner can give the server its **own name and logo**. An early **Android app** is in the repository, not yet in the stores.

### Upgrade notes

- **Six migrations, no mandatory environment variables.** Replace the image or the binary and restart; migrations 0040 to 0045 (relay devices, task pages, projects kept away from AI assistants, automation rules, profiles, workspaces) run on start. There are no down-migrations, so back up first. New optional settings: `OPENTODO_MAPS`, `OPENTODO_MAPS_TILES_URL` and `OPENTODO_MAPS_GEOCODER_URL` (maps), `OPENTODO_STT_URL` and `OPENTODO_STT_KEY_FILE` (dictation through your own speech service), and `OPENTODO_AUTOMATIONS` (automation rules). Upgrade the server before the devices, as for every release.
- **Sample tasks and the start guide's checklist add two additive sync fields** (`sample` on tasks, `start_checklist` in the user's preferences); older servers refuse them as unknown fields and the devices keep their values, older apps show sample tasks as ordinary tasks. No migration.
- **Workspaces add one migration** (`0045_workspaces`: `workspaces`, `workspace_members`, `workspace_invites`, `groups`, `group_members`, `project_grants`) and a column `project_members.via`, added at startup together with one default workspace per account; every existing member becomes a direct grant. No new variable. Back up first, as for every release. An older server ignores the new tables and column, but devices from this version write `workspace_id`, which an older server refuses, so downgrade the server only together with the web app.
- **Reactions and mentions in notes are new sync fields** (`reactions.<account id>` on comments, `note_mentions` on tasks); both stay plaintext with end-to-end encryption. Upgrade the server before the devices: an older server refuses the new fields. No migration.
- **Quick add reads numeric dates in your date format only (breaking).** The device-level choice "Numeric dates like 12/11" under **Settings → Capture** is gone: `12/11` is read day-month or month-day as your date format in **Settings → General → Date and time** says, and with System or ISO as your language says. A device that had fixed the other order reads such dates differently now; the stored choice is ignored and removed. ([Quick add](docs/quick-add.md))
- **Profiles add one migration** (`user_profiles`, `email_changes`); pictures are stored under `<data>/avatars` and are part of backups.
- **Calendar alarms become reminders.** Display alarms set in a CalDAV calendar app turn into OpenTodo reminders the next time the app saves the task, and OpenTodo reminders now appear in calendar apps as alarms. Nothing is converted in bulk; alarms the reminder model cannot express (sound, email, repeating) stay the app's own data.
- **Task pages add one migration** (`task_pages`, `task_page_replies`, `task_page_actions`) and two additive comment fields (`guest_name`, `page_id`); older apps ignore them. Publishing is refused on a demo instance.
- **Maps are on by default.** Task locations show OpenStreetMap maps and search addresses through your server, which fetches from OpenFreeMap (tiles) and Photon (search) with no account data. Set `OPENTODO_MAPS=off`, or switch off **Settings → Server → Maps**, to keep the server from making those requests. Tiles are cached under `<data>/cache/maps` (at most 200 MB, not backed up). ([Maps](docs/self-hosting/maps.md))

### Added

- **Your own name and logo.** **Settings → Server → Branding** lets the owner name the server (up to 40 characters) and upload a square logo: the name replaces "OpenTodo" in the browser tab, the installed app's name, the sign-in screen and the sidebar, and the logo becomes the favicon, the touch icon, the installed app's icons and the sidebar mark, rendered by the server in every size from one PNG or JPEG. The sign-in screen then reads "Powered by OpenTodo". Routes `GET`/`PUT /api/v1/settings/branding`, `PUT`/`DELETE /api/v1/settings/branding/logo` and the public `/branding/logo-<size>.png`; `auth/status` carries `branding`; changes are recorded as `settings.changed`. ([Branding](docs/self-hosting/branding.md), #234)
- **Profile pictures from your sign-in provider.** With the owner's new switch **Use profile pictures from …** in **Settings → People → Single sign-on** (off by default), the picture your OpenID Connect provider names becomes your profile picture when you sign in or link the provider: cropped to a square, scaled to 256×256 and re-encoded, so no metadata is kept. A picture you upload yourself is never replaced, one you remove is not fetched again, and a picture that cannot be fetched changes nothing. Uploading a picture was already there, in **Settings → Account → Profile**. ([Single sign-on](docs/self-hosting/sso.md#profile-pictures-from-the-provider), #233)
- **An interface audit for contributors.** `make ux-audit` opens every screen and the main overlays of the app at 360, 768 and 1280 px, light and dark, in English and German, and writes a contact sheet with the findings ranked: overflow, target size, focus rings, cut-off text, overlapping controls, layout shift, movement under reduced motion, the interface budget and how fast the Inbox shows from the local copy with the server stopped. CI runs it on every code change and uploads the report. A screen inventory keeps the router, the layout check and the workbench in step, and the workbench's accessibility gate now also checks the focus ring on the core screens. The rarely used screens are in the workbench too (sign-in and the other signed-out screens, the invitations, display mode, the public list, Import, Help, the capture screen, a location's page, the About settings and the project and voice dialogs), each with a visual baseline in both themes at phone and desktop width.
- **Completing a task feels like it.** The check fills and pops, the title is struck through and the row folds away while the rows below move up, all within 400 ms; the completion is saved at once, so <kbd>c</kbd> on three tasks in a row never waits and the cursor moves on. A repeating task stays and rolls to its next date. Undo during the moment keeps the row in place. Rescheduling, dragging, bulk actions and Undo slide the rows that stay to their new places (up to 40 rows; changes from other devices never move the list). Dialogs fade and scale in from 640 px and leave faster, the phone sheet and the task panel slide, toasts slide up, and menus fade in and close at once. Phones and tablets tick on completion and on a long-press lift, with a **Haptic feedback** switch in **Settings → Appearance** that follows your account. With the system's reduced-motion setting only colour and opacity fade; nothing moves or scales.
- **Profile: your picture, name and email.** **Settings → Account** opens with a Profile card. Change your name, add a picture (crop it in a circle with the mouse, touch or the keyboard; the browser turns it into a 256×256 JPEG and the server re-encodes it so no location or other metadata is kept), or change your sign-in email: with outgoing email a link to the new address confirms it and the old address gets a notice, without it the change applies at once after you confirm your password. Pictures show wherever initials did (account menu, comments, assignees, members, history) for people who share a project with you. Accounts that sign in only through single sign-on keep the provider's email. ## [0.7.0] - 2026-10-18

OpenTodo 0.7.0 is a hardening release, the result of a review of the whole code base. Your account is harder to take over: credential and administration changes need an admin token or your signed-in session, repeated wrong passwords lock password sign-in for a while, a new server cannot be claimed from the internet without a setup code, forwarded addresses count only from trusted proxies, and **Sign out everywhere** ends every session and token at once. Sync keeps your changes in more of the situations where it could lose them (a device with a wrong clock, a proxy answering in the server's place, signing out during a sync, the history of a newly joined project), large servers and long lists answer faster, backups no longer fill the disk and reach S3 above 5 GB, and the app works better with screen readers and voice control. Templates for fourteen more platforms and a `stable` image tag make installing and updating simpler. Read the upgrade notes first: emailed links now need `OPENTODO_BASE_URL`, and scripts that change credentials need an admin token.

### Upgrade notes

- **Migrations `0035` to `0038`** (attachments of projects shared earlier move to the project; the `admin` token scope; confirmed pages of a joined project's history; an index for dependencies) are applied automatically at startup. Back up `/data` before upgrading: there are no down-migrations.
- **No new environment variable is required, but set `OPENTODO_BASE_URL`** if you have not. Password resets, sign-up confirmations and invitations are emailed only when it is set, and with it set the app must be opened at its host (the sign-in screen names the expected address otherwise). With an https base URL the server also sends HSTS. ([Outgoing email](docs/self-hosting/outgoing-email.md#set-the-base-url-first))
- **Reverse proxies and client addresses.** Proxies on loopback, private or container networks, and `OPENTODO_TRUSTED_PROXIES=cloudflare`, need nothing. If you set `OPENTODO_TRUSTED_PROXIES` to your own addresses, the list now replaces the default: add the new keyword `private` if a reverse proxy on the machine or a container network also connects (for example `private, 203.0.113.10`); a list without any local address shows a configuration warning. A reverse proxy on a public address must be listed, otherwise every visitor shares its address. Behind `OPENTODO_BASE_URL`, open the app at the base URL's host.
- **A new server reached from the internet asks for a setup code** before the owner account is created, unless `OPENTODO_SETUP_TOKEN` is set: it is printed in the log (`docker logs opentodo`). From the same machine or your local network nothing changes, and servers that already have an owner are not affected. ([The setup code](docs/self-hosting/docker.md#the-setup-code))
- **API clients and scripts.** A `full` token is refused on the credential and administration routes with `403 insufficient_scope`: use an `admin` token there (see Breaking). Request bodies are checked strictly, so a field a route does not define is refused with `400 bad_request`. Sign-in answers `429 account_locked` after repeated wrong passwords, and password checks can answer `503 busy`. ([API](docs/api.md#behaviour-notes))
- **Sync** stays compatible: upgrade the server before the devices, as for every release. Changes the server makes itself now come from the device `server:<id>` (one id per server process; webhooks still report the source `server`). Apps from 0.7.0 confirm each page of a joined project's history and correct their clock when the server asks; older apps keep the previous behaviour. One new route: `POST /api/v1/me/sign-out-everywhere`.
- **Give the container time to stop.** On shutdown the server waits up to 20 seconds for its background jobs. Docker stops a container after 10 seconds by default, so set `stop_grace_period: 30s` in your compose file (or `docker stop -t 30`) to let a backup or a digest finish cleanly.
- **Image tags.** The release publishes `ghcr.io/pixari/opentodo:0.7.0` and `:0.7` for `linux/amd64` and `linux/arm64`, and moves `:stable`, which most templates and `docker-compose.yml` now follow. Installs on `:0.6` stay on 0.6: change the tag to `stable` (or `:0.7`) to update. The templates for stores that need a version (CapRover, CasaOS, Easypanel, Helm, Runtipi, TrueNAS, Umbrel) name `0.7.0`.
- **Binary size** is about 28 MB (27 MB in 0.6); the installed app's precache stays at about 4.4 MiB.

### Breaking

- **Scripts that use a `full` token on credential or administration routes must switch to an `admin` token** (breaking for those scripts only; everything else, including reading these settings and lists, revoking tokens and app passwords, and forgetting remembered browsers, works with `full` tokens as before). Such requests now get `403 insufficient_scope`. Create an admin token in **Settings → Integrations → API tokens → New token**, access **Admin** (after confirming your password if asked), or `POST /api/v1/tokens` with `{"scope":"admin"}` from a signed-in session, and put it in the script instead. `ot token create` needs an admin token in the profile too; `ot login` still works. Existing tokens keep their scope. ([API](docs/api.md#token-scopes), [CLI](docs/cli.md#tokens-and-export))

### Security

- **A server can no longer turn end-to-end encryption off on your devices.** If the server says it has no encryption key record (for example after it was restored from an older backup), your devices keep encrypting and pause syncing instead of uploading your content in plaintext. A screen lets you restore the key record from the device, turn encryption off on that device, or sign out. When you turn encryption off on one device, each of your other devices now asks once before following. ([End-to-end encryption](docs/e2ee.md))
- **Encryption key records with unusual settings are refused.** A device only unlocks with a key record whose key derivation strength is within the expected range; otherwise it stays locked and says why.
- **Only the share sheet can hand files to the installed app.** Other websites can no longer store shares in the app through its share target, and a share larger than 100 MB in total is not stored. ([Capture](docs/capture.md))
- **The encryption threat model now notes that the server can show an older version of an encrypted field.** Content stays unreadable and unforgeable to the server, but the app cannot tell when it is served a previous encrypted value of the same field. ([End-to-end encryption](docs/e2ee.md))
- **Pasting into notes is safer.** The rich-text editor's ProseMirror view is updated to 1.42.6, which fixes a published issue with pasted content (GHSA-c8x8-7fp4-3x9w).
- **Links are encrypted with the rest of your data.** Turning end-to-end encryption on now also encrypts the address and name of links you attached before, and turning it off decrypts them again. Each link is replaced by a copy in the new form; the old one moves to the Trash. ([End-to-end encryption](docs/e2ee.md))
- **A damaged or tampered backup archive cannot fill the disk during a restore.** Every file in the archive must have exactly the size its manifest lists, nothing more is written, and the server checks that the listed files fit before it extracts anything. ([Backups](docs/backups.md))
- **Images and release files can be verified.** The container image is signed with cosign (keyless, tied to the release workflow) and carries an SBOM and build provenance; every file on a GitHub release has a build provenance attestation for `gh attestation verify`. ([Running with Docker](docs/self-hosting/docker.md#verifying-the-image-and-downloads))
- **A release is published only when every check passed.** The image, its `stable` tag and the GitHub release now wait for the layout, interface, docs website and template checks too; the image is built, scanned for known vulnerabilities (a critical one with a fix available stops it) and smoke-tested before it is pushed. Build tools and base images are pinned to exact versions, and Dependabot proposes updates weekly.
- **Release binaries and the image are built with Go 1.27.2**, which fixes standard-library vulnerabilities (net/http, crypto/tls, crypto/x509 and others) in the 1.26.0 toolchain used up to 0.6. The `go.mod` toolchain, the Docker build stage and CI use the same version, the image's web app is built on Node 26, and CI fails when the code can reach a known vulnerability (`govulncheck`). People building from source still need Go 1.26 or newer; the `go` command downloads 1.27.2 for them.
- **Security reports go through GitHub's private vulnerability reporting.** See `SECURITY.md`.
- **Forwarded client addresses count only from trusted proxies.** `X-Forwarded-For` (and Cloudflare's `CF-Connecting-IP`) are believed only when the connection comes from a trusted proxy: by default loopback, private and link-local addresses, which covers a reverse proxy on the same machine or a container network. A client that reaches the server directly can no longer choose the address that rate limits, the audit log and the demo limit see. IPv6 clients are limited per `/64`. ([Behind a reverse proxy](docs/self-hosting/docker.md#behind-a-reverse-proxy))
- **Repeated wrong passwords lock the account for a while.** Ten failed passwords, two-factor codes or re-authentications for one account within 15 minutes, from any addresses, lock its password sign-in for the rest of those 15 minutes (the app says so; the API answers 429 `account_locked` with `Retry-After`). A successful sign-in clears the count. Passkeys, single sign-on and existing sessions keep working.
- **A new server cannot be claimed from the internet without a code.** Without `OPENTODO_SETUP_TOKEN`, creating the owner account from a public address now needs a one-time setup code that the server prints in its log when it starts without an owner (`docker logs opentodo`). From the same machine or your local network nothing changes. The code is forgotten once the owner exists. ([The setup code](docs/self-hosting/docker.md#the-setup-code))
- **Stricter origin check with a base URL.** With `OPENTODO_BASE_URL` set, browser requests must be addressed to its host, so a page under another name that resolves to your server is treated as cross-site.
- **Secure cookies over https, and HSTS.** Cookies are marked `Secure` whenever the request arrived over https, also without a base URL. With an https `OPENTODO_BASE_URL`, responses send `Strict-Transport-Security` for one year (no `includeSubDomains`, no preload).
- **Password checks can no longer exhaust a small server.** Registration, project invite links and password resets check the sign-up policy, the invitation or link and the setup token before the password is hashed; at most twice the CPU count of password checks run at once, and a request that waits more than a second gets 503 `busy` with `Retry-After`. Sign-in refuses passwords over 1,024 bytes without checking them. ([API](docs/api.md#behaviour-notes))
- **Sign-in timing no longer tells accounts without a password apart** (single sign-on and demo accounts): they cost the same check as an unknown address.
- **Live-update streams are capped:** 10 per account (a new one closes the oldest), 20 per client address and 2,000 per server; beyond the last two the stream is refused with 503 `busy` and the app keeps syncing by polling. ([Sync](docs/sync.md#live-updates))
- **One account's push endpoints cannot hold back everyone's reminders.** Reminders are sent for 8 accounts at a time with a 20 s budget per account and pass, an account keeps at most 20 push subscriptions (a new one replaces the least recently registered), and a subscription that failed 20 times in a row and delivered nothing for a day is no longer sent to until the device re-enables notifications. ([API](docs/api.md#reminders-and-push))
- **Calendar data, MCP batches and calendar subscriptions are bounded.** Reading iCalendar data (CalDAV writes and subscribed calendars) takes time in proportion to its size, and one content line may be at most 64 KB. The MCP endpoint refuses JSON-RPC batches under protocol revisions that no longer have them (2025-06-18 and later), accepts at most 20 messages per batch otherwise, and stops running a batch once its answer reaches 4 MB. Expanding a subscribed calendar's repeating events starts at the shown period, shares one work budget and a 5-second deadline per calendar, and keeps what it found with a warning when it runs out. ([CalDAV](docs/caldav.md), [MCP](docs/mcp.md#limits-and-security), [Other calendars](docs/other-calendars.md#limits))
- **Emailed links always use the server's public address.** Password resets, sign-up confirmations and invitations sent by email are built only from `OPENTODO_BASE_URL`, never from the request, so nobody can make the server mail a link to another site. Without `OPENTODO_BASE_URL` these emails are not sent: the screen says the owner must set it, links to copy keep working, and the owner sees a configuration warning while outgoing email is set up. ([Outgoing email](docs/self-hosting/outgoing-email.md#set-the-base-url-first))
- **Forwarded host headers count only from trusted proxies.** `X-Forwarded-Host` and `X-Forwarded-Proto` are used only when the request comes from an address in `OPENTODO_TRUSTED_PROXIES`. Behind a TLS proxy, set `OPENTODO_BASE_URL` (or list the proxy) so the links the app shows start with `https://`. ([Docker](docs/self-hosting/docker.md#behind-a-reverse-proxy))
- **A shared project's id cannot be taken over.** Sharing a project is refused when another account has a project with the same id, so nobody can lock the owner out of their own project. ([Sync](docs/sync.md))
- **Entity ids are limited to safe characters.** Letters, digits and `. _ : @ + = ~ -`, which covers every id the apps create, so a malformed id can no longer break the lists of a shared project's members. ([Sync](docs/sync.md#operation))
- **Credentials and administration need an admin token or your signed-in session.** A `full` API token keeps everything it could do, except: turning two-factor authentication on or off, recovery codes, passkeys, creating API tokens and app passwords, unlinking single sign-on, changing the password, invitations, user administration (roles, deactivation, deletion, reset links, ownership transfer, two-factor resets), the two-factor policy, single sign-on settings, the encryption key record, the outgoing-email, backup and assistant settings that hold secrets, and a hosted server's whole-server archive download. Those now take a session (re-confirmed in the last ten minutes where they already asked for that) or a token with the new **`admin`** scope, which can only be created from such a session. A leaked `full` token can no longer turn off your second factor, mint more credentials or issue reset links. ([API tokens](docs/api.md#token-scopes))
- **Single sign-on never links the owner, admins or accounts with two-factor authentication or a passkey by email alone.** Those accounts sign in as usual and link the provider in Settings → Account → Linked accounts; until then the provider sign-in is refused with "This account is not linked yet". Members without a second factor are still linked by their verified email. ([Single sign-on](docs/self-hosting/sso.md))
- **A password reset forgets remembered browsers**, so every browser asks for the two-factor code again, whether the link came from **Forgot password?** or from an administrator.
- **Sign out everywhere.** Settings → Account → Session → **Sign out everywhere** (`POST /api/v1/me/sign-out-everywhere`) ends every session of your account, this one included, forgets remembered browsers and revokes your API tokens and CalDAV app passwords at once. It is recorded in the audit log as `sessions.revoked`. ([Account security](docs/account-security.md#sign-out))
- **Every route declares who may call it.** Each API route is registered with one policy (anonymous with a reason, signed in, owner, owner and admins, accepted token scopes, re-confirmed session, refused on a demo), replacing the separate owner and session checks that disagreed (outgoing email refused tokens while single sign-on accepted them; both now follow the rule above). A test fails for any route without a policy and checks the demo restrictions against it.

- **Changes that can't be made say why.** Moving a task under one of its own subtasks, a move a section does not allow, or a change that fails to save now shows the reason at the bottom of the screen instead of doing nothing.
- **Only one tab syncs.** With the app open in several tabs, one of them syncs for all; the others show its status and take over when it closes. Moving between lists no longer starts a new sync each time. ([Sync protocol](docs/sync.md))
- **Changes held back by a server limit are no longer lost or sent twice** when they are offered again at the moment another change is held.
- **A title you are typing stays as you type it** when another device renames the task at the same time; leaving the field saves your title.
- **The browser is asked to keep the app's data**, so it is not deleted when space runs low or after weeks without a visit.
- **A device with a wrong clock no longer freezes a field.** When a device's clock runs more than five minutes fast, the server now asks it to correct its clock; the app stamps the change again and uploads it, so the edit is kept and later edits from other devices win as they should. The app also stops correcting its clock once the system clock is right again. Older apps keep the previous behaviour. ([sync](docs/sync.md#clocks))
- **Two server writes in the same millisecond no longer cancel each other.** Every change the server makes itself (API, assistant, reminders, imports, email) now uses one clock, and the server's device id carries a suffix per server process (`server:<id>`; webhooks still report the source `server`).
- **History of a newly joined project is resent when a page is lost.** The app confirms each page of a shared project's history it applied, and the server sends a page again until it is confirmed.
- **Deleting an account unassigns its tasks in other people's projects**, as when a member leaves, so no task keeps a deleted person as its assignee.
- **Notifications and webhooks are no longer lost when the app disconnects during a sync.** Once a change is saved, its notifications and webhook deliveries are recorded even if the request was cut off.
- **Impossible due dates are refused.** A due date that does not exist (such as 31 February) or a time outside 00:00–23:59 is no longer accepted, so it cannot break recurring tasks and reminders.
- **A database migration that would have been skipped is reported.** The server records which migration files it applied and logs an error at startup for a file it never ran; the repository checks refuse a new migration not numbered above those on the main branch.
- **Lists and syncs no longer wait for slow writes.** The server reads through a small pool of read-only database connections next to its single writer, so task lists, syncs and exports answer while a large import, purge or backup step is being saved. A second server process on the same data directory (during a rolling update) now waits its turn to write instead of failing with "database is locked".
- **Joining a shared project no longer slows down your lists and first sync.** On a server with many users, listing tasks and the first sync of a member of a shared project read every user's data; they now read only the projects you can see (on a 300,000-change server, a task list went from about 460 ms to 54 ms, or under 4 ms for a member with little data of their own). The server also refreshes its database statistics at startup and daily.
- **Saving many dependencies at once no longer stalls the server.** A sync that adds hundreds of "blocked by" links to a large project used to hold the database for many seconds (about 10 s for 1,000 links on 10,000 tasks), delaying everyone else; it now takes a fraction of a second. Completing a task also checks for unblocked tasks faster. A new database index is created on start.
- **The activity feed loads faster on projects with a long history.** Each page now reads only the recently changed tasks it needs, and never the notes documents, instead of the whole history of every shared project. The events shown are the same.
- **Large public project pages, syncs for members of many projects and CalDAV clients cost the server less.** A public link view no longer runs one query per task, listing memberships no longer runs one query per project, and CalDAV multiget and calendar queries no longer scan the whole account for each task asked for.
- **A new hosted instance always gets its setup link.** In multi-tenant hosting, creating an instance whose record could not be read back right away replied without its setup token; the reply now keeps the record just created.
- **More reliable tests and checks (for contributors).** Every API test now checks the status of each response, `make check` and CI run staticcheck, the server and the web app share one list of synced and encrypted fields that both test suites check, and a randomized test with replayable seeds checks that devices with wrong clocks converge after a project is shared. The demo's "busy" API message now starts in lower case like the others; the app shows its own text.
- **Snooze and Dismiss from a notification follow the corrected clock.** On a device whose clock is off, the notification actions now stamp their change with the same server-corrected time as the app, so the change is neither overwritten by an older edit nor refused; a change the server does refuse stays queued for the app's next sync instead of being dropped.
- **Enter in a filter moves to its results right after the view opens.** Pressing Enter before the view had read your tasks left focus in the query field; it now moves to the first result once they are loaded.
- **Clearing a property you just added removes its chip.** After adding Estimate (or another property) from the Add menu and setting a value, clearing it again now removes the chip instead of leaving an empty one.
- **For contributors: the web app is linted for React hook mistakes.** `make check` now runs ESLint with the Rules of Hooks and effect dependencies (`make web-lint`), and the web tests fail on any network request they did not mock.
- **Long lists stay fast.** Moving through a list with `j` and `k` now redraws only the two rows involved instead of every row (in a 2,000-task project, 2 rows per key press instead of 2,000).
- **Edits and syncs read your tasks once, not once per panel.** The list, the sidebar counts, the project header, labels, calendars and the task panel now share one live copy of your tasks, so each change or sync reads the stored tasks once instead of about six times with a project open.
- **Large lists group and encrypt in linear time.** Grouping a list by project and showing a section's completed tasks no longer slow down sharply with thousands of tasks, rows no longer each open their own lookup when encryption is on, and turning encryption on or off no longer re-reads all your data for every batch of 200 items.
- **Commands work the same in every language.** In German, French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese, the Vim `i` command now puts the caret in the task's title, and moving through the list with `j` and `k` releases a project left focused in the sidebar.
- **Viewers of a shared project can no longer skip, detach, schedule or estimate its tasks by keyboard.** Shift+K, Shift+O, `s` and `e` now do nothing on a task you can only view, like the other editing keys, instead of making a change the server then undid.
- **Today and the sidebar counts move to the new day at midnight** while the app stays open.
- **Confirmations are the app's own dialogs.** Making someone the owner, leaving a project, revoking an invite link, undoing an import and requiring two-factor authentication ask in the app instead of the browser's pop-up. Adding a link to a note (Ctrl/Cmd+K) opens a small dialog that accepts only `https://`, `http://` and `mailto:` addresses, instead of silently dropping anything else.
- **The Logbook's "done" count and the estimate field's example are translated.**
- **The calendar's hour lines follow the theme**, including high contrast and dark.
- **More list actions say why they failed.** Bulk keys, park, duplicate, "this occurrence only", adding a subtask and completing from the panel now show a message when the change cannot be saved.
- `SECURITY.md` now says, as the server does, that the audit log takes the client address from the last `X-Forwarded-For` hop (the one your proxy appended), with `OPENTODO_TRUSTED_PROXIES` for proxies in front of it.
- **Old repeating events in subscribed calendars.** A daily event that started decades ago (before about 1972) showed no occurrences; it now shows every day of the period.
- **Attachments move with a project when it is shared.** Files added to a project's tasks before sharing it stayed visible only to the owner. Sharing now moves them with the tasks, and on upgrade the server moves the files of projects shared earlier; members' devices fetch them on their next sync.

- **`ot`: new exit code 5, "not permitted".** Refusals for scope, role, a switch, encryption or read-only mode (`insufficient_scope`, `forbidden`, `not_permitted`, `writes_disabled_by_owner`, `writes_disabled_by_user`, `encryption_enabled`, `read_only`) exit 5 instead of 1, so scripts and agents can stop instead of retrying. **Migration:** scripts that test for exactly `1` should also accept `5`; scripts that test for non-zero are unaffected. The op log and the REST API are unchanged.

([AI assistants](docs/mcp.md#letting-an-assistant-make-changes))

### Upgrade notes

- **No mandatory configuration.** Every new environment variable is optional and every feature that talks to the outside world is off until configured. The complete list is in the [README](README.md#configuration).

- Reminders (`reminder` entity, relative or absolute) with in-app notifications, snooze and dismiss, and web push implemented with the standard library (VAPID, RFC 8291); key pair in `<data>/push_vapid.pem`, `OPENTODO_PUSH=off` to disable. [Reminders](docs/reminders.md)
- Time-zone-fixed due times: optional `tz` key in `due` (IANA zone, only with `time`), resolved by `internal/zoned` and the web app against shared vectors. [Zoned due values](docs/sync.md#zoned-due-values)

**Collaboration**
- Project sharing with viewer, editor, admin and owner roles: the op log is partitioned into sharing scopes, ops are authorised against the author's membership at receipt and refused ones are reported per op. [Sharing scopes](docs/sync.md#sharing-scopes), [API](docs/api.md#sharing)
- Task assignment (`assignee_id`), the Assigned to me view and in-app notifications (`GET /api/v1/notifications`). [Assignment](docs/api.md#assignment-and-notifications)
- Threaded Markdown comments (`comment` entity) with `@mentions`, and an activity log derived from the op log, per task and per project. [Comments](docs/api.md#comments-and-activity)
- Activity feed across shared projects with unread counts (`project_prefs` entity, personal scope only; `GET /api/v1/activity`). [Activity feed](docs/api.md#activity-feed)
- Invitations: single-use, expiring links in Settings → Members. [Invitations](docs/api.md#invitations)
- User administration: admin role, deactivation, deletion, password reset links, owner transfer, own password change and `opentodo reset-owner-password` on the host. [User administration](docs/api.md#user-administration)

**Integrations and data**
- Data import of the app's own export, CSV and JSON exports of other task managers, with preview, duplicate detection and undo (`OPENTODO_IMPORT_MAX_MB`, default 20). [Import](docs/import.md)
- Scheduled backups to a folder, S3-compatible storage or WebDAV with retention, optional `OTBK1` encryption, `OPENTODO_BACKUP_*` presets and `opentodo restore`; sync responses carry a log `generation` so devices re-send newer edits after a restore. [Backups](docs/backups.md)
- Signed outbound webhooks with a durable queue, retries, delivery log and SSRF protection (`OPENTODO_WEBHOOKS_ALLOW_PRIVATE` preset). [Webhooks](docs/webhooks.md)
- CalDAV two-way sync under `/dav/` with per-device app passwords (`otap_…`), off until the owner enables it (`OPENTODO_CALDAV` preset); new task field `ical_extra` keeps unmapped iCalendar properties. [CalDAV](docs/caldav.md)
- Read-only MCP endpoint at `/mcp` for AI assistants (five tools, `opentodo://` resources), off until the owner enables it (`OPENTODO_MCP` preset). [MCP](docs/mcp.md)

**Security and access**
- Passkeys (WebAuthn) when `OPENTODO_BASE_URL` is https; verification in `internal/webauthn`.
- Two-factor authentication (TOTP) with recovery codes, remembered browsers, an owner policy (`none`, `owner`, `all`), step-up re-authentication for sensitive routes and `opentodo mfa-reset` on the host.
- Single sign-on with one OpenID Connect provider (PKCE, JWKS verification with the standard library), optional `OPENTODO_OIDC_*` overrides, account linking and an SSO-only mode with owner break-glass. [SSO](docs/self-hosting/sso.md)
- Audit log of security events with retention, NDJSON export and opt-in client addresses. [What is logged](SECURITY.md#what-is-logged), [API](docs/api.md#audit-log)
- Optional end-to-end encryption of task content per account (Web Crypto, AES-256-GCM, passphrase plus recovery key); routes under `/api/v1/encryption/`. [E2EE](docs/e2ee.md)

**Launch**
- Demo mode for a public demo instance: `OPENTODO_DEMO=true` creates a hidden owner and a shared demo account shown on the sign-in screen ("Try the demo"), seeds realistic data through the op log, wipes and reseeds it every night at 03:00 UTC (devices recover through purge markers, sessions stay), shows a banner, and refuses sign-up, account changes, integrations, import and encryption with 403 `demo_restricted`; no outbound requests. [Demo instance](docs/self-hosting/demo.md)

### Changed

- Deleting a project only tombstones the project; its tasks keep their `project_id` and come back when the project is restored from the Trash.
- API tokens have a scope: `read` tokens are refused on every write route and on `/api/v1/sync` (`403 insufficient_scope`); existing tokens stay `full`.
- `/api/v1/sync` responses gained additive fields: `rejected`, `memberships`, `purged`, `trashRetentionDays` and `generation`; requests may carry `tzOffset`. Served ops carry `actor`. Older clients ignore them.
- REST collections and the export include projects shared with you (with read-only `role` and `owner_id`), and the export gained `labels`, `views`, `reminders`, `sections`, `comments` and the `encryption` key record. The export format version stays 1.
- `ot` embeds Go's time zone database so zoned due times resolve on hosts without one (Windows, distroless).
- Server dependencies: `github.com/fxamacker/cbor/v2` (passkeys). Web dependencies: `qrcode-generator` (two-factor QR codes), `yjs` and the `prosemirror-*` packages (rich-text notes, lazy-loaded). Everything else uses the standard library and existing packages.

### Fixed

- Imports no longer drop sections, recurrence and reminders. An own export now round-trips its sections, reminders and saved views with their ids (so section placement, recurring series and reminder links stay intact) and every task field, with recurrence stored canonical; reminders whose time has passed undelivered are imported dismissed so no stale notifications go out. Comments are reported in the preview but not imported (a comment keeps its author), and assignees other than you are dropped with a warning. CSV and the JSON formats of popular commercial task apps map recurrence strings (rule grammar, RRULEs, common spellings, structured objects; anything else is a preview warning), reminders (absolute and minutes before the due time) and sections. The `sections` import option gained `section`, now the default (`label` keeps the old behaviour); the preview counts new sections, recurring tasks and reminders; undo also removes imported sections, reminders and views. [Import](docs/import.md)
- Reminders on tasks in a project in the Trash (or under a sub-project whose ancestor is in the Trash) no longer fire, neither as web push nor in the app. Restoring the project brings them back: a reminder whose time passed while the project was trashed is delivered once, late, like after server downtime. [Reminders](docs/reminders.md#trash)
- `/api/v1/sync` pull pages, including the history replay for a newly joined shared project, are capped at about 4 MB of op values (always at least one op), so a batch of large rich-text notes no longer produces one huge response; `hasMore` asks the device to continue. [Sync](docs/sync.md#paging)
- Purging a task, a project (with its tasks) or emptying the Trash by retention now removes the task's CalDAV resource name in the same transaction; migration `0024` removes rows earlier purges left behind. [CalDAV](docs/caldav.md)
- A large import sent one webhook delivery per task. Imports and their undo now produce one `import.completed` or `import.undone` delivery per subscribed webhook with counts and project ids; ops written by an import no longer emit `task.*`/`project.*` events, so a webhook that wants imports subscribes to the new events. Ordinary writes keep their per-event deliveries. [Webhooks](docs/webhooks.md#imports)
- Opening the app at a different host than `OPENTODO_BASE_URL` made passkeys fail with the browser's "origin is not authorized for the provided RP ID"; the app now explains which two hosts differ and what to change, and Settings → Security shows the host passkeys are tied to (also `passkeyDomain` in `GET /api/v1/auth/status`). In `docker-compose.yml` an `OPENTODO_BASE_URL` you set yourself now wins over Coolify's generated `SERVICE_URL_OPENTODO`.
- Dropdown menus (view, section, project and label menus) could open mostly off screen on phones when the header wrapped; they now shift back inside the screen when opened.
- The keyboard-shortcut help could not be scrolled or closed on phones (the panel grew past the screen on iOS Safari). It is now a full-screen sheet on small screens with a pinned close button, the overlay scrolls instead of the panel, and every other dialog overlay scrolls when its content is taller than the screen.
- Clients skip ops for entities they do not know instead of failing the whole batch, so an installed PWA that has not refreshed yet keeps syncing when the server gains a new entity.
- A reference to a deleted, foreign or not-yet-received section or label is displayed as "No section" or hidden without writing a repair op, so arrival order can never overwrite a newer edit.

### Security

- Sign-up is closed after the owner account; new people join by single-use invitation links or, if allowed, single sign-on.
- Sensitive routes (new API tokens, app passwords, invitations, user administration, two-factor and SSO settings, encryption key changes) require a sign-in or re-authentication within ten minutes on cookie sessions (`403 reauth_required`).
- Rate limits on sign-in, registration, passkeys, the second sign-in step, re-authentication, invitation and reset previews, single sign-on, comment creation, admin actions and failed `/mcp` and CalDAV authentications.
- Webhook delivery refuses loopback, private, link-local, metadata and other internal targets unless the owner allows private networks, and never follows redirects.
- CalDAV accepts only app passwords; `/api/v1` refuses them. `/mcp` accepts only bearer tokens and checks `Origin`.
- Credentials, secrets and audit details are kept out of logs and the audit log by an allow-list; secrets for backups and single sign-on live in `<data>/secrets/` (mode 0600), outside the database and backups.
- See [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) for the full model.

### Known limitations and verification follow-ups

These features are implemented and covered by automated tests (unit, integration and in-process protocol tests), but could not be verified against the real thing in this environment:

- **CalDAV** with real clients: Apple Reminders, Thunderbird and DAVx⁵ (Android tasks apps).
- **Single sign-on** against a real OpenID Connect provider such as Authentik or Keycloak (tested against an in-process provider).
- **Email to task** against real mail providers' IMAP servers (tested against an in-process IMAP server).
- **MCP** with real AI assistant clients.
- **Voice capture** with a real microphone in Chrome and Safari.
- **Web push** on real devices and push services.
- **Passkeys** on real devices and authenticators.
- **Share target** behaviour in Firefox (and other platforms listed in [docs/capture.md](docs/capture.md)).
- **Touch drag and drop** (board, manual ordering, calendar) on real touch devices.

Other known limitations: CalDAV does not translate alarms into reminders (alarms survive round trips in `ical_extra`), MCP has no stdio bridge, comments are Markdown text rather than rich text, the browser extension is not published in the stores, and quick add does not parse recurrence or reminders. See the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md#future-ideas).
